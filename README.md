# Effect of Florida's Pill Mill Policy on Opioid Prescriptions and Overdose Mortality

## Project Overview
This project evaluates whether Florida’s 2010 anti-pill-mill policy was associated with changes in opioid prescriptions and overdose mortality compared with similar states that did not implement the same policy during the study period. Using county-level data from 2006 to 2014, the analysis compares Florida with Colorado, Nevada, North Carolina, and Wisconsin to assess whether trends in prescribing and overdose outcomes diverged after the policy intervention.

## Data and Methods
The project uses three main data sources: county-level opioid shipment data, overdose death data, and county population estimates. Prescription data were standardized into Morphine Milligram Equivalents (MME), merged with population data, and used to calculate prescriptions per capita. Overdose mortality data were cleaned, aggregated to the county-year level, merged with population estimates, and used to calculate overdose mortality rates per 100,000 residents. The analysis uses pre-post comparisons and a difference-in-differences framework to compare Florida with control states before and after the 2010 policy change.

## Key Findings
The results suggest that Florida’s anti-pill-mill policy was associated with a decline in opioid prescriptions per capita and overdose mortality relative to the control states. In the prescription analysis, Florida’s mean opioid shipment rate decreased from 2011 to 2014, while the control states increased over the same period. The report also found that overdose mortality declined in Florida after policy implementation while comparison states generally continued to increase, supporting the conclusion that the policy may have reduced opioid-related harm.
